Companion to the desk card · NQ / ES

Build the screen once.

Two charts, nine indicators, about twenty minutes. Do it once, save it, and every morning is just opening the workspace.

The layout two windows, one job each

1 · Chart 1 — the structure chart

  1. Open a new Chart window. Set the instrument to NQ or ES (front month) and the period to 5 min. Read the full-size contract even if you execute MNQ/MES — micros print thin, misleading clusters.
  2. Press Ctrl + I to open Indicators. Search the name, click Add to Chart, set it, click Apply. Repeat for the four below. That's the whole loop — you'll do it nine times total across both charts.

Mark the two levels ATAS won't draw for you. Using the horizontal-line tool, add the Globex High / Low (overnight range) and any naked POC — a POC from an earlier session that price has never returned to. Both are prime trap locations. Two minutes, before the open, every day.

2 · Chart 2 — the footprint chart

  1. Open a second Chart window, same instrument, same 5 min.
  2. Press Shift + K to switch it into footprint mode. You can also just stretch the time scale with the mouse until cells appear. A toolbar for footprint options shows up on the left.
  3. Press Ctrl + S for chart settings → Clusters tab. Set Content to Bid × Ask. Volume and Delta modes hide the one thing you need — which side was aggressive at each price.
  4. Still in Clusters → find Bid/Ask Imbalance and set the rate to 300% That's the 3× diagonal comparison — ask at one price against bid one tick below. This is what colours the imbalanced cells.
  5. Press Ctrl + I and add the five flow indicators from the table below.

Do not skip this. Every volume number above is a starting point, not a setting. Watch one full session first and note what a typical cluster looks like versus a genuinely big one on your feed, then set the threshold at the big one. Thresholds set too low light up the whole chart and the signal disappears.

3 · Save it so you never build this twice

  1. Save the chart template on each chart — that stores the indicators and their settings so you can apply the same setup to another instrument in one click.
  2. Save the workspace — that stores both windows, their positions and the instrument. This is what you open each morning. Indicators are saved with chart templates or workspace settings, depending on which you use — so do both and you're covered.
  3. Make a second workspace for ES by applying the same template. Same setup, different thresholds (the lower numbers in the tables above).

4 · The morning routine five minutes before 09:30

  1. Open the workspace. Confirm the front month hasn't rolled.
  2. On Chart 1, read off and mark: prior VAH / POC / VAL, prior day H/L, Globex H/L, any naked POC. Note where the fat HVN shelves and thin LVN gaps sit.
  3. Note where price is opening relative to prior value — inside, outside, or outside-then-back-in. That decides which setups are legal today.
  4. Check the calendar. Flat over FOMC / CPI.
  5. At 10:30, the Initial Balance completes. Add IB High / Low to your levels. Prime window runs 10:00–11:30.

5 · Practise before you size up Market Replay

  1. Open Market Replay and pick a past session.
  2. Choose Ticks + DOM mode. The generated-tick modes are faster but they will not show you true absorption — which is the whole reason you're here. Accuracy over speed for this.
  3. Trade it in a replay account. Run 20–30 sessions, one setup at a time, and check the Trading Journal for profit factor and drawdown.
  4. Keep only the setups you can execute cleanly in real time. A setup you can't trigger under speed is not an edge you own.
Feed check. None of the flow indicators mean anything without aggressor-tagged tick data — Rithmic, CQG or dxFeed. On a delayed or eval feed the imbalances are noise. Confirm this before you spend a morning tuning thresholds.
Version drift. Field names move slightly between ATAS releases. If a setting isn't labelled exactly as above, it's in the same panel — Clusters tab for anything footprint-related, the indicator's own settings window for everything else.
