Order-flow desk card · NQ / ES · ATAS

Trade where someone is trapped.

Odds don't come from a better pattern. They come from taking trades where the invalidation is small and specific, and the payoff has a mechanism behind it — traders who must cover, or an auction that is unfinished and must travel.

1 · Location
Is price at a level where risk changed hands?
→ your pre-market map
2 · Context
Is the market accepting or rejecting price here?
→ open vs prior value
3 · Flow
Is aggression being absorbed, or meeting nothing?
→ ATAS footprint

All three must agree or there is no trade. Everyone has 1 and sometimes 2. ATAS is the only part of your stack that answers 3 — that is the entire reason to pay for it. It turns "a level" into "a level someone is defending with size."

B · Reading the footprint bid × ask at every price

This is the part nobody else has. Each cell shows contracts traded at the bid (sellers hitting) × at the ask (buyers lifting). An imbalance is a diagonal comparison — ask at one price vs bid one tick below — flagged when one side is ≥3× the other. Three stacked in a row is the signal.

The one distinction that matters: big delta with price moving is initiative — go with it. Big delta with price stalling is absorption — fade it. Delta alone tells you nothing; delta versus price progress tells you everything.

D · The three setups each one is a mechanism, not a pattern

REVERSAL · PRIMARY

1 · Failed Auction / Trap

Traders entered on a breakout that failed. They are offside and must cover. Their stops are your fuel.

  • WhereGlobex H/L, prior day H/L, IB H/L, VAH/VAL
  • Triggerthe 1- or 5-min candle that closes back inside the level
  • Entrylimit at / just inside the reclaimed level on the retest
  • Stopbeyond the failed extreme + 2–3 ticks
  • T1opposite side of the immediate range, or POC
  • T2far side of value · next naked POC

What must show — need 2 of 4

  • Cluster Search — an outsized cluster right at the extreme. Someone big took the other side.
  • Delta / CVD — divergence: CVD makes a new extreme, price does not.
  • Stacked Imbalance — now printing in the reverse direction as price comes back.
  • Speed of Tape — spikes on the break, then dies. Aggression spent.
Why the odds tilt. You are positioned against a group with a known, clustered stop location — and your own risk is defined by that same extreme. If price trades back out, the auction was never failed: you're wrong immediately and cheaply.
REVERSAL

2 · Absorption at a Level

Aggressive orders are hitting a wall of passive size. The passive side is usually the informed one.

  • WhereHVN, POC, VAH/VAL, or a level the Absorption indicator marks
  • Triggerfirst candle that closes against the aggression direction
  • Entryon that close
  • Stopbeyond the absorption cluster's extreme — tight, that's the point
  • T1POC / opposite value-area edge
  • T2through the next LVN — vacuum, price runs

What must show — need 2 of 4

  • Big Trades · Smart Tape — large prints on the passive side. Smart Tape re-aggregates split orders so you see true size, not fragments.
  • Cluster Search — one price whose cluster keeps swelling while price stays flat.
  • Delta / CVD — delta strongly positive, price flat or lower.
  • Absorption — the indicator marks the level directly.
Why the odds tilt. You side with size that is patient and willing to work limit orders, against size that just paid the spread and got nowhere. When the aggressors give up, they exit into you.
CONTINUATION

3 · Initiative Continuation

The auction is unfinished. Price crossed an LVN with no defence and must find acceptance further out.

  • Whereafter price breaks and holds outside a level; pullback into the last imbalance zone or the LVN just crossed
  • Triggertrend-direction imbalance reappears while the zone holds
  • Entrylimit at the zone
  • Stopthrough the zone — initiative is over, get out
  • T1next HVN
  • T2measured move / next naked POC · trail behind each new HVN

What must show — need 2 of 4

  • Stacked Imbalance — 3+ in the trend direction on the drive.
  • Footprint / Cluster Statistic — thin clusters through the travelled area. Nobody defended it.
  • Speed of Tape — slows on the pullback, re-accelerates on the resume.
  • Big Trades — no opposing large prints at the pullback low/high.
Why the odds tilt. You are not chasing the break — you are buying the retest of a vacuum the market has already shown it will not defend. If it defends now, your stop is right there.

E · Before you click any "no" = no trade

  1. Price is at a level from my map — not mid-range.
  2. Context agrees with the direction (day type from section C).
  3. Flow confirms — 2 of the setup's four ATAS signals.
  4. I can state my invalidation price in one sentence.
  5. My target is a real liquidity pool — HVN, POC, opposite edge — not an R multiple.
The checklist is the edge. A desk's advantage is not a better signal. It is refusing to act when the conditions are only partly there.

F · Managing the trade

Exits

Scale at T1Half off at the first liquidity pool, stop to breakeven, let the rest run to T2.
InvalidationThe structural price. Never an arbitrary R multiple.
Time stop15–20 min. A trap or absorption trade pays quickly or not at all — if the covering hasn't shown, the mechanism isn't there. Flatten.
NeverMove a stop away from price. Ever.

Risk

Per tradeFixed dollar risk, 0.5–1%. Size contracts off the stop distance so a 40-pt and a 12-pt stop cost the same.
Per dayMax 2–3 trades. Two losses = done.
EventsFlat over FOMC / CPI. The tape lies through releases.
InstrumentRead the full-size NQ/ES footprint, execute in MNQ/MES. Micros print thin, misleading clusters alone.
