Footprint fundamentals

The number says who was impatient. The price says who was right.

Everything confusing about bid × ask comes from one thing: the labels describe where a trade happened, not who wanted what. Fix that and the rest falls out.

1 · What the two numbers actually are

Every cell shows one price and two numbers. They are not buyers and sellers. They are aggressive sellers and aggressive buyers — and the words are the wrong way round from what you'd guess.

2 · Every trade has both sides

1180 × 395 does not mean 1180 sellers and 395 buyers. Somebody bought all 1180 of those contracts too — they just did it patiently, with a resting limit order, instead of crossing the spread.

3 · The answer: big number × price response

A big number on its own is neither bullish nor bearish. Pair it with what price did, and you get four completely different reads. This table is the whole skill.

Read the diagonal. The two boxes that make money are the counter-intuitive ones: heavy buying that goes nowhere is a sell, and heavy selling that goes nowhere is a buy. That is absorption, and it is the single most reliable thing the footprint shows you.

How to judge “price does not move”: compare the last three bars' highs (for a top) or lows (for a bottom). If they sit at roughly the same price while the numbers stay huge — that's absorption. If each one extends further — the aggressors are winning and there's no trade for you.

4 · Why imbalance is measured diagonally

You'll notice ATAS compares a cell to the one diagonally below or above, not the one beside it. There's a simple reason: bid and ask at the same price never compete. A buyer lifts the offer at one price; a seller hits the bid one tick lower. Those two are the real opponents, so those are the two you compare.

5 · Four cells — read them

Cover the right column. For each, say the read out loud before you look.

price made a new low
Sellers winning — bearish, go with. Heavy aggressive selling and it achieved something. No trade against it; look to join a pullback, don't try to catch it.

Sellers absorbed — bullish, fade it. Identical cell, opposite conclusion. All that selling and price won't go lower: somebody is quietly buying every contract. Those sellers are trapped. This is Setup 1.
280 × 1150

Buyers absorbed — bearish, fade it. Big lifting into a high that won't extend. Passive sellers are eating it. This is Setup 2, the trap.
88 × 26

Nobody is home — go with it. Tiny, one-sided, on low volume. This isn't weakness, it's an empty pocket: no resting orders to slow price down. This is Setup 3B.

6 · The three sentences

BID= aggressive selling. Big left number = sellers were impatient.
ASK= aggressive buying. Big right number = buyers were impatient.
PRICEdecides which of them was right. That's your trade direction.
If you remember one thing. Aggression that achieves something = go with it. Aggression that achieves nothing = fade it. You are never trading the size — you're trading whether the size worked.
