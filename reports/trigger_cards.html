The gate — trade or stand down

Run this before you look for any setup. Most of the session ends at “stand down”, and that is the job working correctly — you are paid for the few bars where all of it lines up, not for participating.

Trade when — all of these

  • Price is at a mapped level: vPOC, VAH, VAL, RTH H/L, Globex H/L, or an LVN edge.
  • Clock is inside a trade window (below).
  • 2 of the setup's 4 footprint signals are present.
  • The bar CLOSES to trigger it — a wick through is not a trigger.
  • You can say the invalidation price out loud in one sentence.
  • You are under 2 losses for the day.

Avoid when — any one of these

  • Price is mid-range, between levels. No location, no trade — this is most of the day.
  • Price is inside an LVN, not at its edge. Wait for an edge.
  • Big volume and price still moving — you'd be chasing a trend, not fading a level.
  • Only a wick through the level. No close, no trigger.
  • 3rd or later test of the same level today — each test consumes the resting orders that made it work.
  • Price has accepted beyond your level — it's building volume out there. The level is dead; stop fading it.
  • 11:30–14:00, or the first 5 minutes after the open.
  • FOMC / CPI / NFP inside 15 minutes either side.
  • Signals conflict — e.g. absorption tell, but the day type says initiative.
  • You're on a delayed or eval feed. Every cell number is then meaningless.
The honest ratio. On a normal session you'll get 0–2 of these, not eight. If you're finding a setup every twenty minutes you've stopped requiring a level and started reading the footprint in mid-range — where it is noise.

What “not tradeable” looks like

You cannot judge any of these from the trigger bar. In every case below the last bar looks like a valid setup — real cluster, real delta, real imbalance. What disqualifies it is in the bars before it. So each one is shown as a full sequence: the lead-in, the footprint reads across it, and the delta and volume progression underneath.

REVERSAL

1 · Absorption at a level

Sellers hit hard into your level and get nothing. They're trapped, and they have to buy back.

While you wait

  • Price approaching one of your drawn levels — vPOC, VAL, VAH. Not floating between them.
  • Delta printing heavily negative bar after bar as it falls (−791, −505).
  • Volume rising into the level — that's the aggression arriving.
  • Watch the bid column swell at the level: cells going 900+, 1000+.

The trigger bar

  • Delta still strongly negative (−500 or worse)…
  • …but the bar closes back at or above the level.
  • The low fails to extend beyond the prior bar's low.
  • Enter on that close. Don't wait for a retest — it often doesn't come.

REVERSAL

2 · Trap at the extreme

Price breaks your Globex/RTH extreme, buyers pile in, then it closes back inside. Everyone who bought the break is offside.

While you wait

  • Price pressing one of your session extremes — Globex high/low, RTH high/low.
  • Delta strongly positive into the break (+510) — you want them committed.
  • Ask cells at the new high going 900+. The more that lift, the better the trap.
  • Nothing happens above the level. No follow-through, no fresh highs building volume.

The trigger bar

  • Made a new high beyond the level…
  • …and closed back below it.
  • Bonus: Big Trades bubble prints at the high — size took the other side.
  • Enter short on that close.

Skip it if price closes back above the level after your entry, or if volume keeps building above it — that's acceptance, not a trap. The break was real and the trapped traders you were counting on don't exist.
DECISION POINT

3 · The LVN edge — reject or run

A thin zone is a price the market already rejected once. Its edge is where that judgement gets re-tested. It either holds — and you fade it — or it's accepted through, and price covers the gap fast. You don't guess which. The footprint tells you.

While you wait — before either branch

  • On the 1H profile, mark both edges of every thin zone. The edges are the levels; the middle is not.
  • Price approaching an edge from the fat shelf. Do not pre-commit to a direction.
  • Watch the cells as it arrives. That single bar decides which branch you're in.

A · Edge holds → FADE (the primary trade)

  • Cells at the edge fatten and go two-sided (640 × 612, 810 × 790).
  • Bar volume rises — it's being contested.
  • Delta pushes hard but price won't cross, and the bar closes back into the shelf.
  • Enter the fade on that close. Stop just through the thin zone — a few ticks. Target back across the shelf.

B · Edge accepted → RUN (second best, later)

  • A bar CLOSES beyond the edge — not a wick through it.
  • Cells stay thin and one-sided (88 × 26), volume drops (3K).
  • Best entry is the first shallow pullback that holds the broken edge — the LVN now flips and acts as S/R the other way.
  • Target the far shelf. Nothing in between will stop it.

Session windows — when the screen is worth watching

Same setup, different hour, different outcome. The levels don't change through the day but the participation behind them does, and these signals need participation to mean anything.
